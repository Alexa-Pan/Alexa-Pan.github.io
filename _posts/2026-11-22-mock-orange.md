title: An elegy in 60 minutes
---

**Choisya Ternata (Mock Orange)**

I try to find the simplest  
possible words to say  
how I feel. Nowadays  
I am no Oscar Wilde  
and the OED has been  
left home for eleven  
years. Merriam-Webster  
started pushing ads, so I  
no longer use it on mobile.  
I just take what words still come  
to me like dreams. (Good and
bad dreams. One night  
I dreamt that Buck said  
“I enjoy Ariana songs more than  
any other” and woke up  
to remember I’ll miss  
Live Band Karaoke night. Another  
night I saw an old friend who had  
finished his physics PhD become a stunt double. 
I saw him perform and his
Korean Girlfriend but didn’t  
know who I was.)  
I learned the real name of my Mock Orange  
just now throwing it out. Six months old,  
finally dead, the smallest leaves crispy and
hung though still green.  
I had tried  
to trim away the brown but it keeps  
returning. Even now and after asking  
Claude I have no idea if I  
had killed it by flood or drought.  
The back of the pot said moderate irrigation and  
I was only ever moderate on average.  
I wasn’t paying that much attention.  
I am so sad, so desperately sad.  
A part of me really counted on it not to
Die. I had longed for the “fragrant white  
flowers” again, springs and summers  
in my childhood home  
doing homework by the window.  
A safe castle floating above the top of the  
orange tree, which 杨爷爷 and 杨奶奶 (who lived  
downstairs, and may also be dead?)  
planted before I was born and produced  
to the best of my recollection ridiculously sour  
oranges. I know the perfume of orange  
leaves pinched and crushed in the palm,  
and the sight of the treetop covered  
in January snow. I expect that  
if the university were to demolish the building  
as they had been saying for years  
the orange tree would be the first to go.  
I remember picking out the Mock Orange  
from the SF botanical garden sale  
with Alek. (His plant had long been dead  
when he moved out, and the pot left behind.)  
I was so excited to care for a plant again,  
and the Mock Orange had stood so  
spirited, so green and ready.  
I thought we would make a partnership  
of it as soon as we’d met, though  
I still lingered for a good half hour  
among the plant racks. Turns out  
all I had been choosing  
so carefully then was which spirit to witness  
seeping away, which friend to witness  
dying. All in all, this plant  
put up a good fight, cracking a new  
leaf bud here and there  
amid many months of  
rot, even as I slipped  
increasingly into denial and put it in  
and out of the sun  
like nothing was wrong. I’m sorry  
Mock Orange. Google said “No,  
mock oranges shouldn’t die in winter”,  
But maybe it is better that you  
Won’t see what’s coming.  
A winter of storms and tides, flight  
and uprooting. Spring of my uncertain  
unscrupulous joy.
